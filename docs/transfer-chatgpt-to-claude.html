How to Transfer a ChatGPT Conversation to Claude

Updated August 2026 · 6 min read

You're deep into a conversation with ChatGPT and you want to continue it on Claude — maybe you hit a usage limit, maybe you want a second opinion, maybe you're switching for good. The problem: your conversation is trapped in one interface, and there's no official "send to Claude" button.

There are three real ways to do it. This guide covers all of them honestly, including when each one is the wrong choice.

Method 1: Copy-paste with a handoff prompt (free, manual)

The obvious method works better if you do one non-obvious thing: don't just paste the raw conversation. Claude responds much better when you frame the pasted text as a continuation. Here's a handoff prompt that works well:

You are continuing a conversation that started on ChatGPT. Below is the
full transcript. Do not summarize it back to me — just pick up where it
left off, keeping the same context, decisions and tone.

=== TRANSCRIPT START ===
[paste your conversation here]
=== TRANSCRIPT END ===

Tips that save you pain:

  • Label the speakers. Prefix each message with [User] and [Assistant] as you paste, or Claude may lose track of who said what.
  • Watch the length. Very long chats can exceed what you can reasonably paste. Keep the first message (it usually defines the task) and the recent messages; you can drop the middle.
  • Expect cleanup. Copying from the ChatGPT interface drags along UI text — button labels, citations, widget content — that you'll want to delete by hand.

Good for: occasional transfers, short conversations.
Bad for: long threads, doing it several times a day — the cleanup gets old fast.

Method 2: Claude's native memory import (free, one-time)

In March 2026, Anthropic added an import tool that brings your ChatGPT memories and preferences into Claude: your writing style, standing instructions, project context, facts about you. You run a prepared prompt in ChatGPT, copy the result, and paste it into Claude's memory settings.

It's genuinely useful — and often misunderstood. What it does not do is transfer conversations. It migrates who you are, not what you were talking about. Your ChatGPT threads stay in ChatGPT; the conversation you had this morning cannot be continued through it.

Good for: switching to Claude permanently and taking your accumulated context with you. Do this once regardless of any other method.
Bad for: moving an actual conversation — that's not what it's for.

Frequently asked questions

Does the formatting survive the transfer?

Mostly, yes. Code blocks, lists and paragraphs come through as plain text with structure intact. What gets lost in any method: images, file attachments, and ChatGPT plugin outputs — those aren't text and can't be pasted.

Will Claude actually pick up where ChatGPT left off?

Yes, remarkably well — if the transcript is framed as a continuation (that's what the handoff prompt is for). Claude reads the whole context and responds to the last open question rather than summarizing what it read.

Is it safe to paste my conversations into another AI?

Treat a transferred conversation like any other prompt: it's now subject to the receiving platform's privacy policy. If the thread contains sensitive data, that's true of any transfer method — or of having the conversation at all.

What about moving ChatGPT conversations to Gemini?

Same three options, one difference: Gemini's native import takes a ZIP archive of your exported history — and it isn't available in the EEA, UK or Switzerland. We wrote a separate guide: How to transfer a ChatGPT conversation to Gemini.
